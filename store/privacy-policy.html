Subtitle Overlay: Privacy Policy

Last updated: November 8, 2026

Summary

Subtitle Overlay does not collect, store on any server, sell or share any personal data. Everything it does happens inside your browser, except downloading the subtitle font from Google Fonts and translation: when subtitles are translated, by your click or by automatic mode (on after a new install), their text is sent to Google Translate, and, only if you turn it on, to Google's Gemini API, to Anthropic's Claude API, to OpenAI's API or to a model running on your own computer. If you choose to write to the author, through the form in the popup or on the page that opens after you remove the extension, what you send reaches the author by email.

What stays in your browser

  • Your style settings, presets, the colors you picked last, timing offset, translation language, second subtitle language, translator choice (with your Gemini, Claude or OpenAI API key or local server address and model, if you entered them), interface language, light or dark look, your show / hide shortcut and the sites you turned the extension off on, saved with the browser's extension storage.
  • The subtitles you loaded on a page (and their timing offset), so they come back when you reload the page or visit it again. Only the last 30 pages are kept, and the × on the "Subtitles from last time" card (or Remove in the popup) forgets that page.
  • Subtitle files you put in the translation queue (with the address and title of the page they came from), until you remove them from the queue, and the improved subtitles the extension keeps under "Saved subtitles", for as long as you choose in "Keep improved subtitles" (from one day to forever, or not at all). Both are listed on the queue page, where the × deletes each one.
  • Short notes on who is who in a film or episode, which the AI translator you chose writes before it improves the subtitles, so it can pick the right gender, forms of address and name spellings. They are kept in the browser's session storage and deleted when you close the browser.
  • How many videos you translated and how many lines that was, with a short fingerprint (not the address) of each of the last 100 pages, so a video isn't counted twice. It only decides when the popup asks, once, whether you'd rate or share the extension, and it never leaves your browser.
  • A list of subtitle file addresses the current tab loaded (on some sites, such as YouTube, the caption text itself, because it can't be downloaded again), kept only for that tab and cleared when you leave the page.

Network requests the extension makes

  • The default subtitle font (Vend Sans) and any other Google Font you choose are downloaded from Google Fonts (fonts.googleapis.com and fonts.gstatic.com). This happens the first time subtitles show on a page, and only on pages where you use the overlay. To avoid it, pick a built-in font (System sans, Serif or Monospace). Google receives the usual request data, such as your IP address. See the Google Fonts privacy FAQ.
  • When subtitles are translated (with the Translate button, or by "Translate automatically", which is on after a new install and can be turned off in the popup), the subtitle text is sent to Google Translate (translate.googleapis.com) and the translation comes back. Nothing else is sent with it: no account, no page address. Google receives the usual request data, such as your IP address, and handles the text under the Google Privacy Policy. The translation itself stays in your browser, like any other subtitles you load.
  • Only if you choose an AI translator (Gemini, Claude, OpenAI or a local model) and click Improve, or translate files in the queue with it: before the first part, lines from the whole subtitle file are sent once so the model can write the who-is-who notes above, and then the subtitles are sent part by part. Where that text goes is described for each translator below.
  • Only if you choose Gemini as the translator and click Improve: the subtitle text is also sent to Google's Gemini API (generativelanguage.googleapis.com) with the API key you entered, and the improved translation comes back. Your key is saved only in the browser's extension storage and is sent only to Google. On Gemini's free tier, Google may use the text to improve its models; see the Gemini API terms.
  • Only if you choose Claude as the translator and click Improve: the subtitle text is also sent to Anthropic's Claude API (api.anthropic.com) with the API key you entered, and the improved translation comes back. Your key is saved only in the browser's extension storage and is sent only to Anthropic. See Anthropic's commercial terms.
  • Only if you choose OpenAI as the translator and click Improve: the subtitle text is also sent to OpenAI's API (api.openai.com) with the API key you entered, and the improved translation comes back. Your key is saved only in the browser's extension storage and is sent only to OpenAI. OpenAI says it does not use API data to train its models; see Data controls in the OpenAI platform.
  • Only if you choose a local model as the translator and click Improve: the subtitle text is sent to the address you entered (by default Ollama on your own computer, localhost:11434, or LM Studio, localhost:1234). With a local address nothing leaves your computer.
  • When you show or save subtitles found on a page, the extension downloads that subtitle file again from the same address the page itself used.
  • Only if you fill in "Write to me" and press Send: your message, the email address if you entered one, and (unless you switch it off) the extension version, browser, system and interface language go to feedback.cleedek.com, a small service that emails them to the author. The service does not store or log the message. The email is used only to reply and fix problems. While you type, the draft stays in your browser's extension storage.
  • When you remove the extension, Chrome opens a short "Sorry to see you go" page at feedback.cleedek.com/bye. Its address carries only the extension's name, its version and your interface language, so the page opens in your language. Answering is optional: only if you pick a reason and press Send do the reason, anything you write and the email address if you entered one go to the author by email, the same way as "Write to me". The service does not store or log them.

No other requests are made. There are no analytics, no accounts and no tracking.
