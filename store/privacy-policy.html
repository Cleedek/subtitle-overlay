Subtitle Overlay: Privacy Policy

Last updated: October 31, 2026

Summary

Subtitle Overlay does not collect, store on any server, sell or share any personal data. Everything it does happens inside your browser, except translation: when you translate subtitles, their text is sent to Google Translate, and, only if you turn it on, to Google's Gemini API or to a model running on your own computer.

What stays in your browser

  • Your style settings, presets, timing offset, translation language, second subtitle language, translator choice (with your Gemini API key or local server address and model, if you entered them), interface language, your show / hide shortcut and the sites you turned the extension off on, saved with the browser's extension storage.
  • The subtitles you loaded on a page (and their timing offset), so they come back when you reload the page or visit it again. Only the last 30 pages are kept, and the × on the "Subtitles from last time" card (or Remove in the popup) forgets that page.
  • A list of subtitle file addresses the current tab loaded (on YouTube, the caption text itself, because it can't be downloaded again), kept only for that tab and cleared when you leave the page.

Network requests the extension makes

  • When you choose a Google Font, the font files are downloaded from Google Fonts (fonts.googleapis.com and fonts.gstatic.com). Google receives the usual request data, such as your IP address. See the Google Fonts privacy FAQ.
  • When you translate subtitles (with the Translate button or with "Translate automatically" turned on), the subtitle text is sent to Google Translate (translate.googleapis.com) and the translation comes back. Nothing else is sent with it: no account, no page address. Google receives the usual request data, such as your IP address, and handles the text under the Google Privacy Policy. The translation itself stays in your browser, like any other subtitles you load.
  • Only if you choose Gemini as the translator: after Google's quick version, the subtitle text is also sent to Google's Gemini API (generativelanguage.googleapis.com) with the API key you entered, and the improved translation comes back. Your key is saved only in the browser's extension storage and is sent only to Google. On Gemini's free tier, Google may use the text to improve its models; see the Gemini API terms.
  • Only if you choose a local model as the translator: the subtitle text is sent to the address you entered (by default Ollama on your own computer, localhost). With a local address nothing leaves your computer.
  • When you show or save subtitles found on a page, the extension downloads that subtitle file again from the same address the page itself used.

No other requests are made. There are no analytics, no accounts and no tracking.
