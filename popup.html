      Translate automatically

      Improve with AI

      Subtitles that sound like people talk

      Google translates each line on its own. The AI reads the whole scene, so jokes, names and who is talking come out right.

      In our test, a viewer rated it
      Google4/10
      Local AI8/10
      Free, no accountStays on your computerAbout 8 GB downloadNeeds at least 16 GB of RAM
      1. 1
        Install Ollama

        A free app that runs AI models on your computer.

        Download Ollama ↗

        Open it once it’s installed. This step ticks itself.

      2. 2
        Download the translation model

        Gemma 3 by Google. Once, about 8 GB.

        Or in Terminal: ollama pull gemma3:12b

      3. 3
        Click Improve under the subtitles on a video

      Use LM Studio or another address?

      Ready. Under the translated subtitles on a video, click Improve.

      AI settings

      Get a free key in Google AI Studio ↗ On the free tier Google may use the subtitle text to improve its models.

      Get a key in the Claude Console ↗ Paid by use, billed to your Anthropic account.

      Get a key on the OpenAI platform ↗ Paid by use, billed to your OpenAI account. A ChatGPT subscription doesn’t include it.

      Ollama uses port 11434, LM Studio uses port 1234 (turn its server on first).

      AI messages

      Says over the video when the AI starts and finishes improving. Problems always show.

      Save improved subtitles

      When the AI finishes, the improved .srt goes to your Downloads folder.

      Stores them in the extension, under Saved subtitles. Nothing is downloaded.

      Find subtitles from others

      Looks for a version someone already improved and shared. Only the start of the subtitles’ fingerprint is sent, never the page or the video.

      Use shared automatically

      No click on Use. Google’s version is always one click away.

      Share my improved subtitles

      Only finished versions from a local model with 12B or more, or from a paid one.

      Second subtitles

      A second set at the other edge of the video, in any language or the original. Handy when two people watch together.
